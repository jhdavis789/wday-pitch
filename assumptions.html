Good AI (35%) — what has to be true

The bull branch. By Q4 FY28, the market sees that Workday's AI agents land at scale and customers consolidate spend on the platform.

The drivers and the why:

  • Revenue growth re-accelerates to 18% by FY29. Subscription growth rebuild from 14% FY26 → 17% FY28 → 18% FY29. The mechanic: existing seats grow ~5%, AI module attach (Illuminate + Sana + agents) adds ~10%, new logos add ~3%. This requires AI ARR scaling from $400M FY26 (disclosed) to ~$3B by FY29 — consistent with the current +100% YoY rate.
  • Operating margin lifts from 7.5% to 26%. R&D 27% → 24%, S&M 26% → 23%, gross margin expands 4pts. The lift is mostly operating leverage — opex grows slower than revenue. This is not a cost-cut scenario; it's a growth-funded leverage scenario.
  • SBC stays elevated at 13–15% of revenue. This is the trap in the Good AI case — Buffett, Ackman, Terry Smith all flagged it. Engineering talent retention requires equity. So the bull case re-rates the headline multiple but doesn't fully fix owner-FCF.
  • Buybacks stay modest at ~1.5–2%/yr. Capital goes to growth, not return.
  • Exit multiple 8× ARR / 22× reported FCF. This is high-growth SaaS territory (ServiceNow at ~12× ARR mid-cycle, CrowdStrike higher). 8× is conservative for a re-rated franchise.

Output: - FY30 revenue: $17.6B (1.84× FY26) - FY30 op margin: 26.1% - FY30 FCF: $6.16B (29 margin) - FY30 FCF/share: $24.66 - Target price: $545 - Cumulative cap return: $23/share (only 18% of $129 — mostly capital appreciation, not cash) - Total return: +339% over 4 years (~45% IRR)

The failure mode of Good AI: SBC stays elevated. The 22× FCF multiple has to hold for the math to work. If the market refuses to pay >18× FCF for an SaaS franchise growing 15-18%, the price target collapses 25% to ~$430. Still good, but the asymmetry tightens.

Runs For Cash (35%) — the graceful pivot

Management acknowledges AI doesn't materially differentiate Workday, pivots to operating leverage and capital return. This is the Oracle template.

The drivers and the why:

  • Revenue growth slows from 14% to 4%. New logo flow decelerates as the market gets pulled toward AI-natives at the margin; existing customers don't churn but don't expand either.
  • R&D drops 800bps to 19% of revenue. Maintenance R&D — protect the platform, ship targeted updates, don't build the next generation. Saves ~$1B/yr by FY30.
  • S&M drops 800bps to 18%. Skeleton sales force defending the install base. The expansion sales motion goes away. Saves ~$800M/yr by FY30.
  • SBC drops to 10%. Equity expense compressed via lower hiring + lower stock-grant levels + actual layoffs. Owner-FCF goes from $1.15B FY26 to $3.44B FY30 (3×).
  • CFO margin expands to 40%. Working capital releases from deferred revenue compression + lower receivables growth.
  • Dividend initiated in FY28 at 20% payout, scaling to 35% by FY30. Total dividend per share: $0 → $2.96 → $5.19 → $7.03.
  • Buyback accelerates to 5.5%/yr at peak — net shares retired: 268M → 221M (-18%).
  • Exit multiple 4.5× ARR / 13× FCF. Mature SaaS — think Oracle (~6× rev / 15× FCF), SAP (~5× rev), IBM software (~3-4× rev). Not heroic.

Output: - FY30 revenue: $12.3B (1.29× FY26) - FY30 op margin: 37.1% - FY30 FCF: $4.67B (38% margin) - FY30 FCF/share: $21.15 - Target price: $261 - Cumulative cap return: $53/share (41% of $129 returned in cash) - Total return: +143% over 4 years (~25% IRR)

The unlock that has to land: Eschenbach commits to a public SBC glide path at the FY27 analyst day. If he refuses, this scenario probability drops to 20% and What Goes Wrong picks up the difference. The whole Runs For Cash math depends on the operator actually executing the pivot.

What Goes Wrong (30%) — AI-natives chip in, MSFT ships, mgmt pivots imperfectly

The integrated downside. Combines: AI-natives erode mid-market new bookings; headcount-clause repricing at -5%/yr; Microsoft ships Copilot for HR mid-FY28 and takes ~10% of new evaluations; management cuts hard but not as hard as Run For Cash because Bhusri blocks the most aggressive moves.

The drivers and the why:

  • Revenue declines 7%/yr. Net of price hikes (+12%/yr on retained base), seat compression (-15%), and new-logo win rate dropping materially as AI-native and MSFT-Copilot alternatives chip the funnel. FY30 revenue $7.37B (down from $9.55B FY26).
  • R&D cut to 15% by FY30. Maintenance-only. No new agent development.
  • S&M cut to 13% by FY30. Defending the F500 anchor; abandoning mid-market growth.
  • SBC compressed to 7%. Layoffs do it — headcount goes from 21,070 to ~12,000.
  • Buybacks aggressive at 6.5%/yr at trough prices — shares 268M → 215M.
  • Dividend ramps to 45% payout — total dividend per share over 4 years: ~$13.
  • CFO margin holds at 32-34% — backlog burn-down protects cash conversion even as revenue declines.
  • Exit multiple 3.5× ARR / 9.5× FCF. Cash-cow software multiple — IBM Software at ~3× rev, late-cycle Oracle Apps at 4×. Not optimistic.

Output: - FY30 revenue: $7.37B (0.77× FY26) - FY30 op margin: 43.1% (lifts despite revenue decline — cost-cut > revenue loss) - FY30 FCF: $2.21B - FY30 FCF/share: $10.30 (flat with today's $10.36) - Target price: $112 (vs $129 today) - Cumulative cap return: $41/share (32% of $129 returned in cash) - Total return: +18% over 4 years (~4% IRR)

The genuinely hard bear is worse than this — see wiki/35_hard_bear.md. If you stack the bad outcomes harder (no SBC compression, Microsoft + Deloitte both abandoning Workday, etc.), you get to $55-75 = -50% from here. The hard-bear agent argues that scenario is 10-15% probability. The 30% probability on What Goes Wrong includes a softer version where management still executes most of the cost cuts.

Sensitivity to the third assumption: dividend initiation timing

Workday currently pays no dividend. The Runs For Cash and What Goes Wrong scenarios assume management initiates a dividend (FY28 in Runs For Cash, FY27 in What Goes Wrong). If they don't, all the capital return is via buyback — same math, different optics for the PM:

FY29 (1y late) $51.10 2.0%

Dividend optics matter for the SaaS investor base. Initiating a dividend signals "we are a mature company now" and unlocks dividend-focused funds. Buying back instead delivers slightly more per-share accretion but doesn't reset the investor narrative.

What we don't know — and how the model handles uncertainty

  1. Net retention rate (specific number). WDAY discloses 97% gross retention but does not disclose net retention. Industry context says enterprise SaaS leaders are at 110-120%; sub-100% is the disruption signal. The model assumes ~105% in Good AI, ~100% in Runs For Cash, ~95% in What Goes Wrong.
  2. AI ARR trajectory beyond Q4 FY26. The $400M, +100% YoY number is the starting point. We assume it continues at >50% YoY through FY28 in Good AI; decelerates faster in the other scenarios.
  3. Headcount-clause behavior. Workday contracts include headcount floors but the renegotiation behavior under AI-driven downsizing is empirically untested. The model assumes 0–5% repricing at renewal in the cash scenarios.
  4. Microsoft Copilot for HR ship date. Could be 2027, 2028, or never. The Microsoft threat agent (wiki/80_microsoft_threat.md) is the most uncertain piece. The model gives it ~10% probability inside What Goes Wrong.
  5. Eschenbach execution. The cost-cut scenarios depend on him being a wartime CEO. His VMware/Sequoia background suggests yes; the dual-class structure means Bhusri can block. Underwriting risk.

How to read this model

This is not a precise valuation tool. It is a probability-weighted scenario forecast designed to answer one question: across the range of plausible outcomes for Workday over the next 4 years, what is the expected total return at today's $129 entry?

The answer the model gives is +144% over 4 years (~25% IRR) including capital return. The worst single scenario produces +18%. The genuinely hard bear (see wiki/35_hard_bear.md) produces -50%, at ~10-15% probability — included inside the 30% What Goes Wrong weight.

The numbers are the floor of the case, not the case itself. The case is in the brief — the disclosed AI ARR trajectory, the FDE moat, the time-to-stand-up-a-new-product advantage, the SBC unlock — and the model is what supports the conclusion that even if we're wrong about the case, the entry-price math protects us.
